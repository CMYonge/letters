My dear [[person:631]Percy]

The houses I should prefer for a boy are

the Revd [[person:446]J H DuBoulay]’s Southgate Hill or

the Revd[[person:196] J T Bramston]’s Culvers’ Close[[footnote:2]]

I have a weakness for a clergyman’s house, and besides, I think so highly of both the men and their wives - [[person:2343]Mrs DuBoulay] as I daresay you know was Alice Cornish sister to the [[person:2344]Bishop of Madagascar]. Both she and [[person:2303]Mrs Bramston] are very nice about the boys. Mrs Bramston’s father is [[person:2342]Archdeacon Adey] he /Mr B\\ is [[person:197]the Dean]’s son. I should certainly try to get [[person:2340]George] into one of their houses, though of course it must be a chance. Preference has really so little to do with it that I do not suppose it would be of any use for me to say anything. They had not, when I last heard, discovered anything in [[person:502]Mr Fearon]s house to account for the illness, but I think there must have been, as three boys were ill, the butler died, and the doctor who attended them like wise.[[footnote:3]] I suppose it will be thoroughly investigated when Mr Fearon leaves it. I hope George may get in to College, and that we may be able to meet when you come with him at Election . I think you are quite right in deciding against Harrow

Yours affectionately C M Yonge
