My dear [[person:1187]Miss Poole], I shall be delighted to put in the history of poor Rosa’s dream, only I must let a few [[other:52]numbers] go by first, or I shall by [sic] inundated with dreams. The [[otherbook:483]gates of Paradise] have set one [[person:749]lady] dreaming already, and the [[otherbook:501]Dreams of the Dead] [[person:778]another], and I think I must let those wait a few months or we shall fall into a universal visionary mood—but I do like the story very much and shall be glad to keep it. It is very like a sailor story I know to be true, but the odd thing there was that the man [ . . . ] drowned and having promised to appear to his aunt he did appear to his wife. I wonder if Rosa’s dream though at morning instead of sunset, was right as to real time.

I should think it must have been like that ghost in India, whose appearance prevented some cheat of or by the Insurance company, I forget which.

Yours sincerely C M Yonge
