My dear [[person:1379]Miss Bourne]

Thank you, I wish I saw my way, but I have been five weeks in Devonshire, and I cannot go forth again so soon, as there is a great deal on hand, and lee way to make up before another holiday We have to hunt for an Infant School mistress as our very excellent one marries [[person:1256]the master] and retires.[[footnote:1]] It is good for him, but bad for us. We must get an ex p t - I believe they are plenty and cheap.[[footnote:2]] Yes, [[otherbook:1061]Miss Marjory’s Ways] are very clever. Have you had [[otherbook:1062]the Dragon of the North]? - It is Fouqué like and yet true - and very good [[otherbook:1063]Bernard and Marcia] is good- so is A Double Marriage, but they are both rather lengthy.[[footnote:3]] I don’t like Amélie’s last ‘[[otherbook:1064]Joan Vellacott]’ - it is sound, of course, and there is a beautiful character in it, but she is barbarously killed, and her husband is deluded by a very horrid girl. Married troubles of that sort are not nice to write about.

Dorothy Osborne’s [[otherbook:1065]letters] are wonderful. I cannot think how she got educated. If she had had a Mme de Grignan, she would have been a Mme de Sevigné It is funny to find her discussing the characters in [[otherbook:1066]the Grand Cyrus] &c just as we do modern novels. I wish I could come to tell you about my visit to the [[person:722]Bishop of Bedford], and seeing the People’s Palace - also the reigning story, but I must keep at home for the present

your affectionate C M Yonge
