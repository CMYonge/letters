My dear [[person:1024]Annie]- I can only be quite sure that [[person:798]Mr. Keble] never taught me at my Confirmation anything about Fasting Communion. When he first came monthly celebrations began here at mid-day the last Sunday in the month, his idea then being that he would come over and assist. So Hursley was fortnightly mid-day first and last Sundays; Ampfield began on the third. Then it was begun at Hursley early on the intermediate Sundays, and I remember its being said that the poor women could come to it then.

I am sure he never commended Fasting Communion to me, nor lamented the omission, though I have a dim idea that once when talking about the expedience of the presence of non-communicants, which he deprecated as a rule, he mentioned the wishing to fast as a possible reason with some, but I am not sure. Early celebrations were certainly never insisted on in this church in his time, but [[person:1544]Mr. Wither]\'s refusal to me when I proposed it was after his death-- I do not think it was thought of before. I cannot tell about his own practice, the only time I spent a Sunday at Hursley being when I was very young.[[person:1634]Jimmy Young] would know better. It is quite possible that his habits grew more strict as time went on, but I am quite sure he did not teach me to practise it, and that he deprecated the attendance without communicating as a rule (for I discussed it with him), only wishing for it for children as part of their immediate preparation for First Communion.-

Your affectionate C. M. YONGE.
