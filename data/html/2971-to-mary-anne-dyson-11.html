'[No salutation] Do you really mean that you are thinking of a [[other:52]rival magazine]? I have a great notion it would be a very good thing, and you would make [[person:351]Mary Coleridge] write, and keep her from being sentimental. Also [[person:1606]mamma] goes into it so vehemently that she desires it to be observed that it might be printed very well and cheaply by the man at Winchester who did [[cmybook:10]Shiverydown], a communication which I consider as premature.[[footnote:1]] Did you ever see such a dreadful little note as she has perpetrated to go in this letter? Pray tell the [[person:1061]fellow-slave] that I am going to Plymouth, and ask if she would like to have a chapter on flowers from thence.[[footnote:2]] I send Edith a promised ear of mummy wheat, enough to sow the whole garden I should think.[[footnote:3]] I am glad the curate has got his holiday, I hope it will cheer him up. Our new school-master comes just as we go, which is I think a pity. Amabel is at this moment in the midst of [[cmybook:15]comforting Guy] about his doom; he has just begun to establish an influence over Charles and to develop a soul in her, both very unconsciously. I don\'t think I have thanked you for the reflections on [[cmybook:18]Emmeline]; thanks to both drivers, she wants an infinity of smoothings down.[[footnote:4]] We are reading the [[otherbook:138]Seven Lamps of Architecture], some part very pretty, other by writing fine very nonsensical, other very powerful, and the beginnings of chapters only fit to be in German. Your most affectionate C. M. Y.
