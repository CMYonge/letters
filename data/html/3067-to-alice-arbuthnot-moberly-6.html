My dear [[person:1025]Alice], I hope [[person:1014]George] is feeling the freshness of these nice cool days[[footnote:1]], and [[person:1019]Mrs. Moberly] is contented and happy without the babies, who by [[person:1020]Mary] and [[person:1012]Edith]\'s account must be very funny, especially [[person:1013]Edward]. What a pleasure it will be to see George at Winchester again, and to hear of all your doings, by which I hope ‘[[cmybook:24]The Daisy Chain]’ will profit, as it has a Commemoration in it. ‘[[otherbook:146]Cleve Hall]’ I like very much, I think the best of [[person:1301]Miss Sewell]\'s stories. . . .We had a nice visit from [[person:968]Dora], [[person:114]Kitty], [[person:1024]Annie], and [[person:1017]Johnnie] last week, and we all joined [[person:1544]Mr. Bigg-Wither] and the Miss Yards[[footnote:2]] and walked to Brambridge Gardens; there was a very hot sun and a thunderstorm - not quite enough to satisfy you, but enough to flatter the children with the hope of being rained up at Otterbourne, in which case I think [[person:374]Miss Cowing] would have been in a tolerable state of alarm. I should have liked to have heard that Sea-King lecture.
