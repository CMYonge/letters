'[To [[person:113]Elizabeth Barnett?]]

[[person:926]J.F.O.][[footnote:1]] slept here last night to assist at the opening of [[person:1222]Mr. Randall]\'s church at Clifton,[[footnote:2]] to which we have been this morning. The Bishop of Salisbury, [[person:1015]Dr. Moberly], preached most beautifully about the Shadow and the Image. [[person:1329]Mr. Skinner][[footnote:3]] is also here for it. . . Those who stayed for the luncheon are full of enthusiasm, and say it was most successful, and that the two Bishops spoke in perfection in their several ways; but [[person:2137]Archdeacon Denison][[footnote:4]] seems to have almost demolished poor Dr. Moberly by with the noise he made. This is a holy and beautiful house to be in, with [[person:557]Blanche]\'s almost unearthly goodness and humility, and [[person:560]her husband]\'s princely nobleness. . . . He still reads the lessons in chapel, and with beautiful expression. Just fancy what it was to hear him read the last chapter of Ecclesiastes, the spirit so rising above the infirmities! He wants to build a chapel here for the district, also a private chapel, licensed for H[oly]. C[ommunion]. But the [[person:2289]Rector][[footnote:5]], a very low and slovenly Churchman, will not consent; though the [[person:2288]Bishop] has been talked to by [[person:1512]our Bishop] and [[person:213]him of Ely][[footnote:6]], he will not or cannot abide it. As to the chapel, there came a letter two days ago, saying \' he would do everything in his power,\' but it is much feared that this means only a licence for the Holy Communion, not permitting anyone not in the house to receive. It is celebrated now in the Oratory, but with a sense that it is irregular and might be stopped when nobody is really ill. How Mr. [Vaughan] and the Bishop can take advantage of the scrupulous forbearance they meet with, I cannot think.
