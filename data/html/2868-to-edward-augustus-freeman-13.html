My dear [[person:523]Dr Freeman],

How very kind in you, and as you say what a pity I did not know of it in time The history of the thing is this [[person:653]one of the sons] of my very old friend, Sir [[person:669]William Heathcote] is in [[other:256]Allens firm] - He asked me to write one of their eminent women series and as I know [[otherbook:1036]Roberts’s history] as one knows the Sunday books of one’s youth, I took [[cmybook:199]H More], helped out by her works and the [[otherbook:1019]Mendip annals of Patty’s journals] - and got what help I could from [[other:207]Tyntesfield], the ‘Bleeding Rock’ is in the grounds there, and Nailsea in sight from the windows.[[footnote:1]] I found Roberts had arranged the letters wrongly, by comparison with Patty’s journal- [[otherbook:1037]Wilberforce’s life] and public events -Now the book has been in the printers’ hands about six months. I have only had one sheet in proof for they only print these books when they have nothing else to do - And there is [[person:743]a sharp Editor], who evidently did not like the touches of improvement I put in.[[footnote:1]] So I am afraid that besides \'mon liège est ecrit [\'] I could not even alter or add much.

But I promised to write for the [[other:32]National society] which is dealing in a superior kind of tales for reward books, [[cmybook:203]a story] taking Hannah’s work from the scholar’s point of view, making a hero of some youth interfering with the fighting & throwing constables down coal pits-[[footnote:2]]

I should be very glad to pick up some information and scenery useful in that line, but I can’t begin till next year, and if I can avail myself of your help in that way, I shall be very grateful. I was at Oxford in April, but I do not think you were there then- Was Somerleaze one of Hannah’s villages? Or did they pelt her out

Yours very sincerely C M Yonge

My kind regards to all your party
