My dear [[person:1243]Madam] I have no time for more than to enclose the June [[otherbook:234]Holidays] and thank you for the last received, I don’t think we Hampshire folks are good at traditions we have none of St Swithin but such as are common to all the world. There is a curious little old Church dedicated to him, over a gate way. I believe, in spite of this rain, he is buried at the back of the Altar in the Cathedral in a place called by the Vergers the Holy Hole. Here we dread a wet St Swithin, but in Gloucestershire they desire it and call it the Christening of the Apples[[footnote:1]]

Yours sincerely C M Yonge
