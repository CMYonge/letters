My dear [[person:244]Mr. Butler] Thanks. I wish I felt more worthy of being an Exterior Sister,[[footnote:1]] but I am thankful to be joined to what is good, though I do not think you would care to have me if you knew how I ‘shrink when hard service must be done,’[[footnote:2]] and what a spoilt child I have been ever since I grew up, very nearly useless in anything practical. But I will constantly use the prayer, and I hope whenever I can come to Wantage that I may be admitted.

I wish we could have seen you again. The church is the same still, and has its atmosphere about it, as much I think as possible. I wish the [[person:1541]Wilson]s had been there, they bring breaths of the old times with them, but altogether it was rather like the [[otherbook:537]Tate and Brady] verse that begins

I sigh whene’er my musing thoughts -[[footnote:3]]

When you read your novel, do let it be [[otherbook:536]Nigel Bartram’s Ideal], one of Warne’s Companion Library - shilling books- it is by the little [[person:1520]lady] who showed us over [[other:130]St. Cross], and I think has a great deal in it.

Is [[person:822]Emma] inclined [[other:104]Goslingwards]? Shall I tell the [[person:329]Senior Gosling] to send her the names and rules?

Yours affectionately C. M. Yonge
