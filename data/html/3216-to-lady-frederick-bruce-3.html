My dear [[person:215]Lady Frederick] I am afraid I cannot give you more than a week, and that the 6th must be the last possible day. I believe I am going to look over the MSS. with [[person:1381]Mrs. Sumner] and send them off on the 1st, but we can add your report at the end. I hope you are really recovered from the influenza. People are having it at Winchester, but rather slightly.[[footnote:1]]

I always thought vaguely that the Mays lived somewhere between Malvern and Wales, but I was called to account for having put crayfish into their rivers. I am always a little afraid of specifically localising unless I know a place intimately. Nor am I sure where Bexley was (did I say Swindon for the junction[?]). I meant the watering-place to be on the Dorset or Devon coast, and Rock Quay had Torquay in its eye. I am sorry to say my coadjutors think it will not do to return thither in the [[other:52]Packet.][[footnote:3]] There are not enough kind old friends like you to make the publishers approve. So when I have time I must finish it singly, but I am hurrying up a National Society story, and I want to do a mother\'s meeting set of readings on the services of the Sundays of the year.[[footnote:4]] Mrs. Sumner\'s energies are going into the school-boy education subject.

Yours sincerely C. M. Yonge
