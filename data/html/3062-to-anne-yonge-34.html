My dear [[person:1582]Anne] I must write a word or two before Church. I do not think I dared to expect better than this, and I do hope that at least the suffering is not what it was the day before.[[footnote:1]] It is the Cross at least, and she has been bearing it so in patience and meekness all her days that one thinks of her as one made meet. I am glad that [[person:27]Alethea] is coming to you. I think I am relieved that the violent attack did not come at the periodical time, and that it does seem gentler now. But I don’t know how to write. [[person:1606]Mamma] is well, and liked hearing the N Z journals. The Selwyns left Hursley too early to be seen, so the Miss Palmers went to meet them at Winchester at 9.25. [[person:795]Mrs Keble] was well enough to enjoy her evening with them. They went to the Moberlys, & the [[person:1295]Bishop] took 9 of the little hands together in his, & blessed them – it was all the young ones except [[person:1026]the baby] and [[person:83]Margaret], who was afraid. The [[other:157]Wigram]s pay all the expenses of the Southern Cross till she or such another reach NZ.[[footnote:3]] I say all this because you may be able to care to read it, otherwise it will not trouble you.

I have one of those 4 sermons of the Bishop from himself. Our good old friend [[person:658]Mrs Heathcote] died this morning.[[footnote:2]] She had had some sleepless nights, and [[person:1544]Mr Wither] thought her failing when he saw her yesterday but she was in better spirits & asked him to send her a fast day service, as they had missed having any at the Park. I do not think Mamma is knocking up. She & [[person:1624]Julian] have been looking over the boxes of papers almost all day.

Goodbye my dear Anne. I can only pray for you all. your most affectionate CMY
