My dear [[person:464]Marianne]

I heard this morning that good old [[person:560]Mr. Gibbs] is gone - on Friday night - his flowers fresh in our church. We had a very successful day, and no doubt [[person:869]Amélie] has told you about it[[footnote:1]], the Confirmation afterwards, thirty-five of our children, the girls led off by [[person:1613]Helen], [[person:783]Amy], and [[person:1482]Gerty] and six of the school-girls with such sweet solemn faces, and a Cranbury man who had been baptized on Easter Sunday.[[footnote:2]] Afterwards fifty-four mothers had tea in the school, and [[person:68]Mr. Ashwell] made them a capital speech about what depended on them. So many are my own old girls. One woman is one of seven sisters, and there are and have been twenty-five already at school of the second generation of that family. A good number were communicants next day. I had a feast of talk with Mr. Ashwell, but imagine his materials for [[person:1512]the Bishop]\'s life - he never destroyed a letter![[footnote:3]] He spoke highly of your godson, and enthusiastically of his wife. Well, I think you will get on well with your young [[person:2184]Vicar], and I am sure you will like his [[person:2302]mother]; I never heard any one find a fault with her.[[footnote:4]] No time for more.

Your most affectionate C. M. Yonge
