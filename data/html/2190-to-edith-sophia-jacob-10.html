My dear [[person:749]Edith] It is a sweet little sad face with the Passion flowers, and we have put it in for Lent.[[footnote:1]] The last verses of the Lenten hymn far on in the book seem to suit it so well. Our criticism was that the glory makes rather a strong line against the right, and perhaps next time you come might be a little toned down, but it is after all the mediaeval habit.

The Haughton girl[[footnote:2]] is come. I have not seen her but she is liked so far at the other house. We are just going for our last tea drinking at the farm. [[otherbook:502]Campanella] turns out charming, and very original.[[footnote:3]] I only wish I could put her in sooner. My [[person:126]friend] is much obliged for being put on the scent of the Prince Consort’s speeches.[[footnote:4]] She has all but done her [[other:190]clerical charities] enough to shew them to [[person:1991]Rivington] as a specimen

yours affectionately C M Yonge
