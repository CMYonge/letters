My dear [[person:1520]Florence]

I am glad you finished your journey prosperously, and I hope you have brought home a store of strength for the winter and for the trials.

How one sometimes wishes that one\'s people may never have another worry, and yet I suppose it is all right! I have just lost my most good and wise friend [[person:464]Marianne Dyson]. For more than a year she had been in so utterly feeble and broken a state that one could only dread further loss of faculties, and there was a good deal of wearing though not acute suffering, so that it was really thankworthy to know that rest had come on St. Michael\'s morning. I have known her 35 years, and she has been a great help and blessing throughout my life. Scarcely a story of mine but has been read and discussed with her, and I don\'t know any one I owe so much to after my [[person:1631]Father] and [[person:1606]Mother] and [[person:798]Mr. Keble]. [[person:195]Anna Bramston] was there, being a friend of her companion [[person:869]Miss Leroy]. [[person:198]Mary Bramston] spent last evening here, her farewell before going to Truro.[[footnote:1]] [[person:1462]Gertrude] is better, but cannot walk at all now. I am so glad you are able to \'take up your pen,\' as poor people\'s letters say. I hope the ideas will flow if you do not call them too hard.

Your affectionate C. M. Yonge
