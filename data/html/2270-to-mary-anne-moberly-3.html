My dear [[person:1019]Mrs. Moberly],

Thank you for your kind, sweet, cheering note. It does seem to me truly that it is the burden of the flesh [[person:1606]she] is freed from, so entirely labour and weariness had the mere act of living been to her for months past; but with what sweet smiles![[footnote:1]] I am glad your dear [[person:1025]Alice] so thoroughly shared the peacefulness of the earlier watch, as well as that last trying day, which I trust will have done her no harm. I hope she and the [[person:81]Goddaughter] may be with us on Friday. She, with [[person:1611]George Yonge] and (perhaps) [[person:1599]Duke] and [[person:1582]Anne], are the only ones to be with us here now who were with us before.[[footnote:2]] The others have ‘all passed into the world of light,’[[footnote:2]] - the dear [[person:798]Kebles], [[person:313]Lord Seaton], [[person:118]Warden Barter], my cousins [[person:1615]James] and [[person:1618]John]. All Alice’s life I think she was the most loved and cheering young thing, out of the family, to both my [[person:1631]father] and mother; and how she helped us in 1854! . . . I have been really alone for some time, so the loneliness does not press so much as no doubt it will.
