Dearest [[person:1042]Jay]

If you would write to me once a fortnight how delightful it would be for we do let each other drop fearfully, and as long as my poor [[person:1462]Gertrude] is in her present state I can not go from home unles I can leave [[person:1558]Mary Woollcombe] here. She is here now, finishing a fortnights stay, during which I have been able to get a few days with the Moberlys. Near as they are, I had not been with them for four years, as they always are away when I go about in August. And the [[person:1015]Bishop] is 81, and feeble on his feet, though not in essentials. It is odd that we had a great talk about this investigation of ghosts. Did [[person:367]Caroline Trench] ever tell you of the wonderful things that her nephew [[person:671]Gilbert] saw and heard at Horsepath, near Oxford. It is too long to write here, and she knows all about it. I think [[person:1012]Edith Moberly] has just that Rip van Winkle feeling you describe about Winchester, especially now that [[person:1237]Dr Ridding] is leaving it.[[footnote:2]]

I wish you could have seen the tableaux of the history of Winchester that Lady [[person:1238]Laura Ridding] and others got up last Christmas, beginning with the Lady Elaine in her boat, then William Rufus brought in his cart, and Cardinal Beaufort hearing the news of Agincourt, Col Fiennes stopping the Roundheads from plundering Wykeham’s shrine was best of all. There were about 20, divided among different families who got them up, for the benefit of a house they have for reforming rough girls, and getting them out to service You ask what [[person:182]Alethea] and [[person:1613]Helen] are about. I believe Alley will be married before the end of the year.[[footnote:3]] She has been engaged for rather more than a year to Mr [[person:184]Henry Bowles], who came as a clerical pupil to [[person:201]Mr Brock] before being ordained. [[person:1744]Lady Chichester ]who was one of the Chamberlaynes has just got her daughter’s trustees to offer him their family living, Arlington, where they live, close to Barnstaple, over £300 a year, and 420 people, 100 acres of glebe and a good house It is rather wonderful, he being not 25, but though not exactly delicate, I don’t think he could do hard work. He is 6 foot 3. The letter kindly came on a Sunday when he happened to be here helping Mr Brock who had a bad cold and touch of bronchitis so they had all the delight of it together, and he is going to see the place next week.

I am glad those two poor girls are to have a little change, it will be very good for them. I see you did not send me [a line cut out in MS] I see [[person:164]Susan Blunt] [word cut out] meetings, and I met [[person:1081]Lord Nelson] looking very shaggy in the stalls at Salisbury.[[footnote:4]]

Did you see [[person:28]Mary Morshead]’s beautiful verses about poor [[person:1340]Mr Merton Smith]? [[person:37]Mildred] is to be married on St. George’s day. I am afraid [[person:2358]Mr Stuart] is very delicate. [[person:1184]John Pode] knew Mr [[person:376]Mercer Coxe] in London, and seems to think highly of him.[[footnote:5]] I don’t think I have read anything very good lately [line missing cut out] is by [[person:1566]Miss Wordsworth], the [[person:1565]Bp of Lincoln]’s daughter, and how touching poor [[otherbook:968]Professor Palmer’s life] is.[[footnote:6]]

your most loving cousin C M Yonge
