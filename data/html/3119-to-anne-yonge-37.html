My dear [[person:1582]Anne]- [[person:321]Graham] and [[person:1804]James Yonge][[footnote:1]] went away before we were up this morning, and it would all have seemed like a dream if [[person:1599]Duke] had not been there at breakfast. [[person:1025]Alice Moberly] came out in the fly that fetched us, and spent the whole day with [[person:1606]mamma]; they gave the schools some buns and sugared negus by way of celebration, and I think mamma did very well.

I think we must have made a very pretty procession; [[person:1624]Julian] went into church first with [[person:130]Mrs. Walter] and James, and then when [[person:1461]the Colonel] brought [[person:1604]Frances], we six bridesmaids lined the pretty lych-gate, all hung with festoons of flowers, and closed in behind her.[[footnote:2]] She had been a good deal overcome while waiting at home, and much more in real need of sal-volatile than [[person:1042]Jane] was, and I believe she had a very bad headache all day, but she was quite right as long as she had anything to do, and was very bright and pretty at the luncheon, with little [[person:1463]Herbert] upon her lap. Poor [[person:616]Louisa] was very much distressed, and little [[person:1462]Gertrude] looked so pale, and clung to her every moment she could.[[footnote:3]] There were about thirty-six people at the luncheon, at a table arranged like a T. . . . Julian looked very nice and well, and one longs for their coming home to eat the great piece of honeycomb which [[person:713]Kezia]\'s mother has most appropriately presented....

The school-children scattered laurel leaves and flowers, and the church was very full of people. Julian told me to send thanks for the pretty little obelisk and the two plates---how very well the sweetwilliam is done, and I have a special delight in the white flower at the base of the obelisk. [[person:798]Mr. Keble] is going to give him a big Bible. I have so many letters to write that I cannot go on any longer-

Your most affectionate C. M. Yonge
