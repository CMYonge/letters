My dear [[person:1582]Anne] Thank you for taking all my impertinence so kindly. I hope you will not be very angry with me for being highly delighted with [[person:351]Mary Coleridge]’s prospects, and not even pitying [[person:923]Alethea] so much as [[person:319]Cordelia Colborne], for you must remember that Mary will live very near home and the sisters may see each other every day of their lives, and for Mary’s youth, she is much older at twenty, than many people are at twenty five, besides you will allow me to make some difference in my rejoicings between [[person:18]a man] of whom I never heard any thing but that his father belongs to the Free Scottish Kirk, and one whom everyone mentions with high respect and admiration. I am very glad to have seen [[person:1129]Mr Palmer], so as really to have heard him talk, one day at [[person:1015]Dr Moberly]’s when there was no one else but ourselves, and it has always been my especial wish, that one of my friends would marry a very great good person, and I am sure that wish is fulfilled.[[footnote:2]] I always told Mary she should not marry without my consent, and I think she has it indeed. [[person:1564]Mr Wordsworth][[footnote:3]] had the great news from Mr Palmer and came to tell us the very day I heard it from Mary. She will be at [[other:127]Hursley] next week when I hope I shall hear all about it. I do not think anything ever delighted me more. We are beginning to prepare for [[person:1544]Mr Wither]’s Consecration feast on the 30th, he means to borrow [[person:282]Mr Chamberlayne]’s tent for the occasion, he has the fifth of his eldest brother’s twelve children staying with him by name [[person:1545]Guy], a very nice little boy of nine years old. [[person:1304]Mr E. Sewell] has just been there and brings news that [[otherbook:147]Gertrude], his sister’s new story will very soon be out, it is rather older than [[otherbook:145]Amy Herbert] and is about a Consecration. Miss Sewell was staying with us at the [[other:124]Ampfield] Consecration, I wonder whether it was taken from that. [[person:1624] Julian] comes home next Monday. I am afraid not till quite late in the day. There has been a great sale at Brambridge[[footnote:4]] which has caused a great beautification of our abode, we have a new low book case for the drawing room, to go along under the noble Persian, with marble at the top, and some beautiful green and white silk damask curtains for that same drawing room which were very cheap The Indian Moberlys[[footnote:5]] have had either chicken or small pox at Field House which has prevented the others from coming there I am sorry to say. How glad I am to hear of [[person:1627]Aunt Marianne]. How do your turkies grow? You have never told me what you thought of [[person:979]Eton Charlotte] which I really do want to know. Also I should like to hear how you got on with [[person:64]Dr Arnold][[footnote:6]] The [[person:669]Heathcotes] come home the first week in August. Little [[person:666]Fanny] was here the other day, she is growing tall, and in her irons can walk much better, though she will not attempt it without at hand, or a hold of [[person:367]Caroline]’s frock, to make her feel secure though it affords her no support. Poor [[person:1702]Mason], (Mrs Reeves) is turned out of her school because she cannot teach history and geography, and she has written to Mamma to ask her to find her a new situation[[footnote:7]]

Your most affectionate cousin CMY
