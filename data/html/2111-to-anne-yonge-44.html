My dear [[person:1582]Anne] As we fully expected, the holy and blessed [[person:798]spirit] went to its rest at one o’clock on Thursday morning; the [[person:795]other] gentle spirit is placidly waiting her call to be with him.[[footnote:1]] She slept quietly after having given thanks after it was over, but was much overcome on wakening, and this is the last we know of her. I should feel comforted to know the rest had come, which cannot be far off now. The erysipelas had nearly passed off, and the [[person:230]Bournemouth doctor] ascribes it altogether to the long strain of sorrow upon the weakened frame. I am very thankful for both their sakes, but we feel very desolate. The funeral is to be on Thursday, and [[person:1606]mamma] has written to offer a bed to the [[person:1636]Peter Young]s, in case they should not have room at the Vicarage. [[person:1544]Mr. Wither] has a terrible cold, and I dread Sunday for him.

Your most affectionate C. M. Yonge
