'My dear [[person:669]Sir William]- I am always bothering you about something, and now I want to ask if you would give me a sentence. I want one describing the remarkable and peculiar merits collectively of the Bench of Judges in the [[person:1156]Patteson] and [[person:344]Coleridge] days.[[footnote:1]] I cannot well take it out of the mouth of a Coleridge and a judge, and I do not think I can do it rightly myself.[[footnote:2]] I want to make as full a picture as possible of old Judge Patteson, for he ought to be remembered, and he went for very much in the formation of [[person:1155]his son]. If you would put, as you would in a letter, your view of the high stamp of men who were judges from about 1830 to 1855, it would be what I want expressed. I do not know any one else whom I should like to see doing it but Sir [[person:1129]Roundell Palmer], and I do not know him enough to ask.- Your affectionate C. M. YONGE.
