My dear [[person:263]Miss Smith],

I should be very sorry to let the tardiness of the [[other:52]Monthly Packet] stand in the way of your [[otherbook:269]Two Beauties][[footnote:2]] so I hope you will offer them elsewhere, and find them successful. There is such a quiet prettiness about them that I am sure they ought to do. And I always have a sense of guiltiness is keeping a thing so long by me, so that I shall be quite relieved if you find a readier opening for your Beauties.

I was glad to meet you in the [[other:62]Churchman’s Companion][[footnote:3]] again. I thought it was you before I saw your A C D, and very pretty the little chapter was. Don’t you rejoice in the Early and Latter Rain being over. I think that was the most exaggerated, over wrought story I ever met with.

I have no fears of not being well able, and much delighted to read the opening of the story you are so kindly doing ‘to order’. It will be more comfortable, as I shall not feel afraid to mark anything I doubt of. Which is not a very flattering sentence but I will let it stand in consideration of its truth, and I shall be very glad to see the M. S. as soon as ever it may suit you. We continue our great liking for the [[otherbook:267]Wynnes] but I am seized with a little fear whether they have plot enough. Your short stories have plenty but your long ones somehow want to be so arranged as to rise up gradually into the leading event and then cease. I think the [[otherbook:274]critique] upon Mary in the [[other:99]S R] might not have been made if her discovery had come before, not after, John’s recovery, which was the dénouement. But I do not think I have told you that Frank is a very decided favorite in two houses.[[footnote:4]] One little girl even wished he was her brother! In which I cannot say that I joined

yours sincerely

C M Yonge
