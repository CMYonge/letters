My dear [[person:1587]Charlotte]

I cannot tell you how much pleasure [[person:1599]Duke]’s letter gave me yesterday morning[[footnote:2]] and the whole Sunday that has passed since has only encreased [sic] the enjoyment of thinking of your future. It is so very pleasant to me that my own first god child, who has always seemed my god child above all my subsequent ones, should be committed to him whom I have known the best of all my cousins – and honored for his exceeding goodness and kindness from the time we were little children together. How comfortable and happy a thing it is to think of you as belonging to the dear Puslinch family, and working with them at Newton - it seems one of the happiest lots that can be, and as if no home could be more peaceful and promising in all that can lead to goodness and help one in doing right. What a gain it will be for [[person:692]Frances] and your brothers[[footnote:3]] to have another such brother – and how much help and comfort it will be to [[person:1626]Mary] and [[person:1582]Anne] to have you always at Newton to be ready for some of the tasks that have pressed heavily of late.

I am writing in a hurry, for the Moors[[footnote:4]] have been here and interrupted, so that I cannot say as much as might be said upon our great gladness, and all the others have run off their several ways, so that I have no collection of messages for you, but we all do most heartily rejoice and wish you joy.

your affectionate cousin

C M Yonge
