My dear [[person:263]Miss Smith], I am ashamed and concerned at not having answered you sooner. If I had known of any thing more likely to suit your purpose than Mr Ridley’s [[otherbook:488]book], I should have done so, but on Saturday I waited to see whether Sunday would inspire me with any conjecture, and I am afraid I must confess that then I forgot it My experience of school work is altogether country and of girls, and I do not know what has been put forth for boys of a little more cultivation. I suppose [[otherbook:489]Steps to the Altar] would be too far advanced. I have often thought a guide to using [[otherbook:494]Bp Wilson] would be best of all, but I am afraid there is no such thing. There are newer books, such as that [[otherbook:490]Weekly preparation] which [[person:798]Mr Keble] sanctioned with his initials, or that in Mr Brett’s [[otherbook:491]Churchman’s Guide] but these are adapted to Weekly Communions and Mr Brett’s book is too large and has much to puzzle young boys in it. I wish I could have helped you better nor do I know a form for coming in and going out of Church. One was written for me in my first prayer book by my [[person:1631]Father], but where he took it from I never knew. I had afterwards these printed from it to put into the school children’s Prayer books, and I send you a specimen. I see you want rather a manual for daily life than a Companion to the Altar as I thought you meant at first. Would not one of those little books with a text and a thought for each day in the year be more likely to be read and kept up than anything else. The [[otherbook:492]best] is by the late Arthur Acland Troyte Esq. but I cannot recollect its name though I think his name would find it at [[other:112]Masters]’s. There is another called ‘[[otherbook:493]The Brook in the Way]’ by [[person:929]Bp Mackenzie]’s youngest [[person:589]sister]. I think what could be read daily with the year would longer have an influence than a book read straight through

yours sincerely C M Yonge
