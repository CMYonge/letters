My dear [[person:2234]Miss Walker] Did people in 1800 think as much about war as we do? In fact I think they had a respite then, while the Peace of Amiens prevailed. I knew the old [[person:542]Dean of Winchester ]who had been at a levee of the [[person:2207]First Consul] in that year with [[person:2208]Sir James Mackintosh]. I made my niece look at him (the Dean) he was 90, by way of making a link

I have a [[otherbook:752]Peacock at Home] with the badly painted pictures you describe /1807\\, but the drawings were [[person:2206]Mulready]’s and are excellent the poem is Mrs Dorset’s.

[[other:233]Griffiths and Farran ]republished it without the paint, and one sees how good they are. The [[otherbook:753]Butterfly’s ball] by Roscoe was the first but the Peacock is much cleverer. The [[otherbook:754]Lion’s Masquerade] is not quite equal to the others, nor is the [[otherbook:755]Elephant’s Ball]. They had all fallen into the way of imitations

yours sincerely C M Yonge
