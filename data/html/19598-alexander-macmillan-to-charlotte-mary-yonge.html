'Dear Miss Yonge,                                                                   Sept 6 1866 We shall look forward to the [[cmybook:69]Chaplet of Pearls] beginning as soon as either[[otherbook:469] “Old Sir Douglas]” or [[otherbook:1129]“The Silcotes”][[footnote:1]] cease. It will be, I venture to think, one of your most popular books, and ought not to be the worse for a little mellowing. I am going down to Scotland with[[person:940] my wife] tomorrow morning, and will be away for a fortnight. After that I hope to be at home all the winter. We shall be very glad to see any of the worthies when you have done them. I quite hope that [[person:941]Mrs Daniel] is on the way of recovery. She is with us at present at [illegible]  of [illegible]  during our absence. I hoped to send you’re a/c for the year with this. [[person:379]Mr Craik] will in a few days. I have several other matters which I want to talk to you about when I come back. I will write you very soon. Very faithfully yours A. Macmillan
