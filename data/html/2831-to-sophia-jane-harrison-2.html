My dear [[person:638]Mrs Harrison] Thank you so much for my [[person:2022]God daughter]’s photograph. Alas! I have been a very bad Godmother to her, never having a chance to come in her way, but I go so little from home and when I do, it is always to my own people in Devon.

I have not been to London even for three years! Unluckily I just missed [[person:2326]Mrs Bland] when she was staying with the Bakers at Winchester. By the by, my youngest niece, [[person:388]Joanna], spends from Monday to Friday every week under [[person:95]Agnes Baker]’s teaching, and very good it is for her.[[footnote:2]] How young things do grow up. I have one [[person:182]married niece] now- her [[person:184]husband] is an excellent young clergyman in Devonshire, and her [[person:1624]father] and [[person:1604]mother] are just gone to stay with her, but it does not seem as if there were to be any grand children.[[footnote:3]] You must rejoice in beginning again with two sets so near you.[[footnote:4]] I have heard Grandparents call it the most delightful relationship in the world- all the pleasure without the responsibility, and I think daughter’s children are more delightful than son’s, because there is less chance of differences of opinion about them!

I had a little correspondence with Mrs De Winton a little while ago about the Mother’s Union Please give my love to dear [[person:1517]Margaret Wilcox], and tell her how sorry I am to hear she is such a sufferer.[[footnote:5]] There are sad changes in that family since they struck me as all so good, and bright and happy in those years long ago

yours affectionately C M Yonge
