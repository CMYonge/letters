My dear [[person:1566]Elizabeth]

I ought long ago to have written to you though you told me not, I fully meant to have done so, I was so delighted with the [[otherbook:854]book], but we have had a sad time of illness with poor [[person:1462]Gertrude] She got better for a fortnight, and then came all the old troubles, and now she has another abscess under her knee and I do not think will be up for a long time to come. But I do like your little book greatly, and I think your Lincolnshire people must be very intelligent to have called it out[[footnote:1]] Have you seen [[person:198]Miss Bramston]’s book, [[otherbook:852]the Carbridges] it is very pretty, and puts me in mind of many things. The eldest daughter has come out so like [[person:1025]Alice Moberly] and the father and mother are so excellently done. I have known people so like them. We are all snow today and I fear it bodes a hard winter. Poor [[person:1404]Warden of New College], it seems like a break up, and what a grief to [[person:377]Mr Coxe] it must be.[[footnote:2]]

Your affectionate C M Yonge
