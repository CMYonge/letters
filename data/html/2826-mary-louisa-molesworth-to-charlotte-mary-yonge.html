Dear Miss Yonge

I had the proofs of [[otherbook:993]‘One Sunday Morning’] some time ago & corrected them carefully. There were several small errors - in themselves small but rather important as affecting the sense, but I daresay the printers will have made all right. I want to know if I may send you another [[otherbook:998]short article] I have by me just now. It is not original, being a free translation from the French of Madame de Pressensé; the original appeared recently in a French serial not known in England. It was beautifully written in French & I don\'t think it reads like a translation in English. It is the history of a sort of French ‘city arab’, and I think it cd. be rather new to English readers. Madame de Pressensé gave me leave to do what I liked with my translation, but I should like it to appear before very long, for her sake. The French title is ‘Le Parigot’, I have called it ‘Felix, an outcast’. Shall I send it to you? I think it cd. easily divide into two parts if too long for one.

My friend [[person:379]Mr. Craik] told me the other day that you had asked him if I was the Mrs. Molesworth who had written [[otherbook:999]‘Carrots’] &c. Perhaps I shd. have mentioned this to you when I wrote first, but there is no other woman of the name who writes, so I did not think of it.

And in one sense it makes me all the more pleased that you like my sketch, without knowing me.

Believe me yours very truly [[person:1028]Louisa Molesworth]

I have stupidly mislaid your address. Will you kindly let me have a word in reply as soon as you can.
