'My dear [[person:113]Lizzie] - I can\'t help sending you this letter, it is so curious. The man appeared here last summer to pick up incidents about [[person:76]Miss Austen][[footnote:1]]. I could not tell him anything but dear old [[person:669]Sir William Heathcote]\'s recollection of her as Mrs. Candour at a Twelfth-day party.[[footnote:2]] They use her for a classic at one of the American universities, and examine in her! It must be fun to hear them! By the bye, I have had two letters from a Hindoo Professor, one Guopna (I think), asking elucidations of some bits of slip-slop in [[cmybook:51]Golden Deeds], which it seems is a class-book at Bombay and posed the poor professors. To have one\'s bad grammar come round in that way is a caution! Do you know, when it was fresh, [[person:1078]Dr. Neale] wrote to thank me for [[cmybook:15]Guy], for making him not only so good but so real, Well! [[person:464]M. A. D]. and [[person:798]Mr. Keble] were at the bottom of Guy, so no wonder. You know how M. A. set me to write the contrition of a good man who had not shot anyone by accident.[[footnote:3]] I have just finished the [[cmybook:207]story of Sabinus] and Eponina I told you I was doing, or rather of their slaves, a Gentile who is born and bred a slave, and a highly educated self-satisfied young Jew, caught in Galilee. The Gentile becomes a Christian, and the Jew despises him, and thinks he can be as good without. They are both caught and tortured to tell where to find their master. The Gentile is silent and dies of it. The Jew speaks hardly.
