My dear [[person:263]Miss Smith]

I am sorry to say [[otherbook:936]your story], even with a little abridging, comes to 63 pp, and as that is nearly a quarter of the Christmas no. I had to give it up with another which I liked almost as well, but was 53 pp, so I am afraid I must keep it waiting for the end of [[otherbook:947]Paul and Virginia] and put it into the regular [[other:52]M P]. I like your Dulcibella very much and am so glad to have met you again in the [[other:62]Churchman][[footnote:1]]

Query? Am I an optimist or is 40 years of work in the same place more encouraging than 10? I believe it is, because one sees the disappointments come round again, and the general mean go on. I am quite sure that the good is more intelligent good, and the average at a higher level. In 1834 came [[person:1544]a clergyman] here who found 12 communicants of whom the greater number were of my [[person:103]grandmother]’s household. Only one of these 12 (not of that house) was alive when he left us, his last Easter Sunday having (I think 75). Our population then 700. His [[person:478]successor] was here 10 years, and died last February, and there were 135 Easter Communicants, the population being now 859 owing to a new suburb to the village. I don’t think I much believe in the old simplicity, I think it was often very rough and cross

yours sincerely C M Yonge
