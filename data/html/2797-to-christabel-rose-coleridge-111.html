My dear [[person:329]Christabel]

I should think on the whole that it was intended to decline the book except at your expense. I do think the resemblance to [[otherbook:930]Don John] is a disadvantage though you have treated it in such a different way.[[footnote:2]] And had not [[person:1992]Low] something to do with Don John, either through [[other:73]Good Words] or [[person:741]Miss Ingelow]. I am on the whole afraid that as I see in the [[other:75]Guardian] that Mr Mallock says that the age has got past the spirituality of Tennyson, so it is getting past the quiet style of domestic Churchey novels– not that there is much of that in [[otherbook:964]the Lot]- but it is the undercurrent.[[footnote:3]] [[otherbook:663]Amethyst] may do better, but I wouldn’t begin her – the book I mean – with the Sisterhood scene. Shall you try anywhere else?

I should think [[person:1667]S T C] was horribly difficult to do properly.- The old unmitigated hero worship was one thing, and the Froude & Carlyle method another and to strike the balance between the two is uncommonly hard.[[footnote:4]]

We have and are having a course of diversions for Christmas here- a Band of Hope[[footnote:5]] Tree comes off this evening

your affectionate C M Yonge
