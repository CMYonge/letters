Dear [[person:744]Mr Innes] Thank you for your kind letter, I suppose there will always be rubs of opinion when three people representing different generations of thought work together if in general principle they accord; and I know I am apt to despise popularity more than perhaps is fair in fellow workers to whom it is more important

As long as there is nothing irreverent tending to ‘Higher Criticism[‘] or to trenching on delicacy I am satisfied and when I have been worried it has been from fear of some of these things - [[footnote:1]]

My object always was to make the [[other:52]Packet] a handmaid to the Church, and I think in the main, it is the same with you.

I am sorry if I was inadvertently discourteous about the [[cmybook:225]Outlook][[footnote:2]]

I am afraid [[person:329]Miss Coleridge] is very unwell No doubt she has written to you that she is gone to Malvern.

I once asked you to send on [[other:220]London Society] but if you can stop its coming I should be glad. The story my [[person:1462]friend] was reading turned out so badly that she never wishes to see it again. A good deal is a specimen of what a serial ought not to be.

Yours truly C M Yonge
