Dear [[person:379]Mr Craik]

Thank you for the Wilberforce extract.

It occurs to me that if the [[cmybook:189]Jubilee history] were put out without illustrations, and with more of the religious element than would be fit for the [[other:68]English Illustrated Mag.] but would be liked by my own special world of readers, the publication if cheap, might answer /moderately\\. But you will judge when you have looked at the chapters I sent. They go about half way.

You would rejoice me by republishing [[cmybook:17]the Herb of the Field]. I should like to have the old copy to look over, for the only one I have was interleaved and illustrated by [[person:1606]my mother], and I could not let it go away.

I do not think there is much wrong in it but perhaps I shall think otherwise when I bring critical eyes to it again.

There are two other sets of papers which I wrote for Mozley’s [[other:79]Magazine for the Young], and I should like much to see published separately. ‘[[cmybook:357]Householders], which is on shells and quite as entertaining as well as newer than the Herb of the Field, and [[cmybook:301]Thoughts on Pictures] - being descriptions for the youthful mind of notable works of art, and their stories. I could add a few more.

I think it would tell more now than when it was written - as it was too much beforehand with the cultivation of art.

[[person:1344]Mr W. Smith] has all the back vols of the Mag for the Young, where these are.

If you are looking up my old books there are some I should like to resuscitate, if you can find out what became of [[other:148]Groombridge]’s books (I believe he failed) He had a series of Magnet stories, for which I wrote four[,] three little plays - [[cmybook:35]the Mice At Play], [[cmybook:41]the Strayed Falcon] and [[cmybook:54]the Apple of Discord] - and a story [[cmybook:46]The Sea Spleenwort], each making a little book, for the plays (historical) are embedded in a story.[[footnote:1]] I think I had £5 for the copyright of each, but they have never been reprinted and I should think could easily be bought back if they can be traced.

Yours truly C M Yonge
